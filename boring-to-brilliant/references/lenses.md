# The 12 Lenses

Each lens forces a different route away from "here are our features". Use at least 6
per session. For each, a short description, the question to ask, and a mini example
from an unglamorous product so the pattern is clear.

---

## 1. Make the invisible visible
Most technical value is invisible: it's the failure that didn't happen. Dramatize it.
**Ask:** What would you see, hear or feel if this product weren't there?
**Example (server monitoring):** A billboard showing a dark office at 3am with one
lit phone: "This is what 'no alerts' looks like."

## 2. Translate the number
A spec means nothing until it's in a unit the buyer lives in: hours, coffees, salaries,
football pitches, family dinners.
**Ask:** What does this number equal in the buyer's world?
**Example (inverter efficiency):** "That extra 1.2% is the electricity bill of your
warehouse's break room. Forever."

## 3. Name the villain
Give the problem a face, a name or a personality. The enemy should be the status quo,
not a competitor.
**Ask:** If the problem were a character, who would it be?
**Example (invoice automation):** "Meet Excel_FINAL_v7. He's been running your
accounts payable since 2014."

## 4. Change the hero
Stop making the product or company the hero. Make the installer, the night-shift
operator or the facility manager the hero, and the product their sidekick.
**Ask:** Who gets the credit when this works?
**Example (industrial sensors):** A series celebrating maintenance technicians who
"fixed it before it broke".

## 5. Zoom in / zoom out
Go absurdly small (one component, one second, one electron) or absurdly big (a city,
a decade, a country's grid).
**Ask:** What's the story at 1000x magnification? At 1/1000?
**Example (cable manufacturer):** "Every Tel Aviv traffic light, every night, runs
through 4mm of us."

## 6. The day without
Imagine a world, a day or a building where the category disappeared.
**Ask:** What breaks first?
**Example (backup power):** "Day 1 without storage: the hospital chooses between the
MRI and the elevators."

## 7. Borrowed world
Steal the format, language or rituals of a totally unrelated, more exciting world:
sports, dating apps, cooking shows, true crime, fashion week, nature documentaries.
**Ask:** How would [unrelated world] talk about this?
**Example (battery cells):** A nature-documentary voiceover following "the rare
evening migration of solar energy from roof to socket".

## 8. Honest confession
Admit the boring truth, the limitation or the industry's dirty secret. Candour is rare
in B2B, which makes it stand out.
**Ask:** What would we say if we stopped pretending?
**Example (compliance software):** "Nobody has ever been excited about audit trails.
Until the auditor showed up."

## 9. The insider ritual
Find the in-joke, the jargon, the daily ritual only people in this job know, and
celebrate it. Insiders share content that makes them feel seen.
**Ask:** What does only a [job title] know?
**Example (solar installers):** "Things installers hear on every roof", a recurring
LinkedIn series.

## 10. The unexpected proof
Prove the claim in a stunt, a test or a demo nobody expects from this category.
**Ask:** How could we prove it in a way that's fun to watch?
**Example (rugged inverter):** Run a café espresso machine off it in the Eilat summer
for a week, and livestream the temperature.

## 11. Flip the timeline
Tell the story from the future (10 years in), or from the past (what the buyer will
regret not doing).
**Ask:** What will the buyer say about this decision in 2036?
**Example (energy storage):** A letter from the facility manager of 2036 thanking
the one from 2026.

## 12. The tiny moment
Find the one small, specific, human moment where the product matters, and build
everything around it.
**Ask:** What's the 5-second moment when someone notices this product?
**Example (smart meter app):** The moment the factory owner opens the app at a wedding
and sees the plant is fine, and goes back to dancing.

---

## Notes on using lenses

- Two lenses can combine (Villain + Borrowed world = a true-crime series about energy waste).
- The first idea under each lens is usually what a competitor would also think of.
  Write it down, then push for a second.
- Lenses 8 (confession) and 10 (unexpected proof) usually score highest on Ownability.
- Lenses 2 (translate the number) and 12 (tiny moment) usually produce the most useful
  everyday copy for product pages and sales decks.
