# Boring → Brilliant: 100 kW commercial string inverter

**Assumptions:** No customer case study yet, so every customer-result claim is marked
[needs verification]. Market is hot-climate (the 60°C spec matters).

## The translation

| Feature | Function | Benefit | Business outcome | Human stake |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Smart I-V curve scan | Tests every string remotely | Finds faulty panels without climbing the roof | Fewer site visits, faster fault fixing | The installer stops losing Saturdays to "the system seems low" calls |
| Works to 60°C without derating | Keeps full output in extreme heat | Produces most on the hottest days | More kWh exactly when tariffs peak | The owner's best month isn't quietly eaten by the weather |
| 10 MPPTs | Handles roofs with many orientations and shading | Each roof section works independently | Fits awkward roofs, more panels per roof | The EPC can say yes to the messy roof a competitor rejected |
| 98.5% efficiency | Loses less in conversion | More of the sun becomes money | Higher yield over 25 years | The CFO sees the number match the proposal |
| IP66 | Sealed against dust and water jets | Survives desert dust, coastal salt, cleaning | Lower maintenance | Nobody has to explain a failure that "shouldn't have happened" |

## The insight
EPC installers want to be known for systems that just work, but every fault, even one
caused by a single cracked panel, becomes *their* problem and *their* weekend, so they
secretly need a way to find problems before the client calls.

## The cliché to avoid
Blue sky, shiny panels, a sunrise, and "maximize your yield with reliable, high-efficiency solutions."

## The top 3

### 1. The Saturday Back: Change the hero
**The idea:** Stop selling the inverter and start celebrating the installer's time.
Remote I-V scans find the faulty panel from a laptop, so the truck doesn't roll for a
"the system seems low" call. Content follows installers on a Saturday they *didn't*
spend on a roof.
**Who it's for:** EPC installers (technical buyer, and the real recommender).
**Headline:** Find the broken panel from your couch.
**LinkedIn hook:** "Last summer, one installer drove 340 km to find a single cracked
panel. [needs verification: real story] / This one found it from his phone."
**Ad line:** Fewer roof calls. More Saturdays.
**Visual:** An installer's work boots by the door on a Saturday, a kid's football
match in the background, a phone notification: "String 7: panel 14 underperforming."
**Proof needed:** A real installer's story with numbers (km saved, hours, number of
avoided visits). Screen recording of an I-V scan.
**Boring → Brilliant:** "Smart I-V curve diagnosis for intelligent O&M" → "Find the
broken panel from your couch."

### 2. Hottest Day, Best Day: Belief to break
**The idea:** Everyone in hot markets accepts that summer heat costs production. This
inverter holds full output to 60°C, so the hottest afternoon is the most profitable
one, exactly when peak tariffs apply.
**Who it's for:** The factory owner / CFO (economic buyer).
**Headline:** Your best day of the year is the one everyone else complains about.
**LinkedIn hook:** "It was 46°C in the Arava yesterday. / Most inverters on those
roofs were quietly cutting output. Ours were working the hardest."
**Ad line:** Built for August.
**Visual:** A thermometer at 46°C next to a live output graph, both peaking at the
same moment.
**Proof needed:** Side-by-side output data on a real hot day vs. a derating unit
[needs verification]; the datasheet derating curve.
**Boring → Brilliant:** "Full power output up to 60°C ambient" → "Built for August."

### 3. The Roof Nobody Wanted: Insider ritual
**The idea:** A recurring series about ugly roofs: three orientations, a chimney,
a neighbor's tree. Roofs other installers said no to. 10 MPPTs make them work.
Insiders love roof stories, and it gives installers something to share.
**Who it's for:** EPC installers; secondarily owners with complicated buildings.
**Headline:** We collect roofs other people reject.
**LinkedIn hook:** "Three angles. Two chimneys. One very stubborn palm tree. /
Four installers said no to this roof. Here's what it produces now."
**Ad line:** Bring us your worst roof.
**Visual:** Drone shot of a chaotic rooftop, each section labelled with its own MPPT
and kWh.
**Proof needed:** 2–3 real installations with permission from the client, plus yield data.
**Boring → Brilliant:** "10 MPPTs for flexible system design" → "Bring us your worst roof."

## Quick wins
1. Change the product-page H1 from "High-efficiency commercial inverter" to "Built for August."
2. Start a monthly "Roof of the Month" post using one installation photo and three facts.
3. Add one slide to the sales deck: "What happens on a 46°C day" with the derating curve vs. a typical unit.
